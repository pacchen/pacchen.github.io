Chip design / Tapeout

My first tapeout.

Paul (Yi-Chia) Chen · October 16, 2026

My first tapeout was an Intel 16 nm chip we built for USC’s Advanced Chip Design and Tapeout class. Our six-person team designed a BERT-layer accelerator, and I led the team through the RTL-to-GDS flow and gate-level verification.

The accelerator supports configurable 4-, 8-, and 16-bit QKV datapaths to explore the tradeoffs between numerical precision and energy efficiency. We took the design through implementation and SDF-annotated gate-level simulation, achieving timing closure at 1 GHz.

As course teaching assistants, we also aggregated all of the students’ designs into a single top-level chip. We connected the blocks using a simple star network and completed the implementation of the integrated chip.

The top-down approach brought its own challenges. In particular, clock insertion delays were imbalanced across the different blocks, making chip-level clocking and timing closure difficult. Those integration challenges helped motivate our later Chipstitch line of work on scalable, cost-efficient silicon prototyping.
